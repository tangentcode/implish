Regression tests for https://github.com/tangentcode/implish/issues/21.
See docs/evaluator.md for the rules.

* TEST issue-21-lines : the two lines from the issue
#+begin_src
> rev x: 1 + ! 3 , + 10 * x
13 22 31
> a: 1 + ! 3 ; rev a + 10 * a
13 22 31
> a: 1 + ! 3 ; rev a , + 10 * a
13 22 31
#+end_src

The third line failed before with "expected number or vector, got: LST".

* TEST rtl-dyad-chain : a dyad takes everything to its right
#+begin_src
> 10 - 5 - 2
7
> 2 * 3 + 4
14
> (2 * 3) + 4
10
> imparse '[1 - 2 - 3]
-[1 ; -[2 ; 3]]
#+end_src

* TEST rtl-prefix-term : a prefix verb takes only the next term
#+begin_src
> rev 1 2 3 + 10 20 30
13 22 31
> rev (1 2 3 + 10 20 30)
33 22 11
> 1 2 3 + 10 20 30, rev
33 22 11
> echo[1 + 2]
3
#+end_src

To give a monad a whole expression, use ( ), [ ], or a comma.

* TEST rtl-recursion : recursion needs brackets around the argument
#+begin_src
> fact: {ite[x < 2; 1; x * fact[x - 1]]}
{ite[x < 2 ; 1 ; x * fact[x - 1]]}
> fact 5
120
#+end_src

=fact x - 1= would be =(fact x) - 1=, so the argument goes in brackets.

* TEST rtl-set-word : a set-word takes everything to its right
#+begin_src
> x: 2 + 3 * 4
14
> 1 + y: 2 * 3
7
> y
6
#+end_src

* TEST rtl-arg-order : arguments run right to left
#+begin_src
> x + x: 5
10
> o: 0
0
> +[o: o + 1; o: o * 10]
1
#+end_src

The right argument runs first, so =x= has a value when the left side runs. In the projection, =o: o * 10= runs first (o is 0), then =o: o + 1=.

* TEST comma-clause : a comma threads only the clause next to it
#+begin_src
> x: 5 ; 1 2, + 1
2 3
> x
5
> 7 ! 3, + 1
Error: [project] !: valence error: expected 1 args, got 2
> y: 9  z: ! 3, + 1
1 2 3
> y
9
> z
0 1 2
#+end_src

In =y: 9  z: ! 3, + 1= the clause before the comma holds two statements (=y: 9= and =z: ! 3=). Only the last one is carried. In =7 ! 3= the =!= is postfix after =7=, so =3= is an extra argument.

* TEST comma-partial : a comma can fill in missing arguments
#+begin_src
> + 1 2 3, 4 5 6
5 7 9
> g: {x + y * 2}
{x + y * 2}
> g 3, 5
13
> imparse '[+ 1, 2]
+[1 ; 2]
#+end_src

If the left clause gives a verb that still needs arguments, the clause on the right gives them.

* TEST comma-errors : a comma needs a clause on both sides

> 1 ,

#+end_src

* TEST data-block : [ ] blocks are data and are not run
#+begin_src
> [1 + 2]
[1 + 2]
> count [a b c]
3
> [1 2; 3 4]
[1 2, 3 4]
> [q: 1]
[q: 1]
> get[`q]
?q
> type? [undefined-word]
lst!
#+end_src

Inside a block, numbers side by side form one strand, and =;= and =,= separate items.

* TEST comma-set-verb : a set-word that stores a verb keeps it a verb
#+begin_src
> f: {x + 1}, 5
6
> f 10
11
> p: + 1, 2
3
> p 10
11
> (g: {x * 2}) 4
8
#+end_src

The value of a set-word is the value it stores. If that value is a verb, the clause after the comma gives its arguments.

* TEST nested-groups : deep nested ( ) groups parse in linear time
#+begin_src
> rev (rev (rev (rev (rev (rev (rev (rev (rev (rev (rev (rev (rev (rev (rev (rev (rev (rev (rev (rev (rev (rev (rev (rev (rev (1 2 3)))))))))))))))))))))))))
3 2 1
#+end_src

The parser parses each ( ) group at most once per statement. Before this fix, each level doubled the work: 20 levels took about 2.6 seconds, and 25 levels took about 30 times as long.

* TEST comma-get-verb : a get-word of a verb is a verb in a group or a comma carry
#+begin_src
> f: {x + 1}
{x + 1}
> (:f) 5
6
> :f , 5
6
> :f
{x + 1}
> type? :f
ifn!
> add: {x + y}
{x + y}
> :add , 2 , 3
5
> n: 7
7
> (:n) , + 1
8
#+end_src

The parser looks up the word of a get-word to find the arity of its value. A get-word alone, or as an argument, does not call the function.

* TEST comma-defines-verb : a word that a comma clause defines is a verb in the next clause
#+begin_src
> f: {x + 1}, 5, f
7
#+end_src

The clause left of a comma runs before the next clause is parsed. So the last =f= is known as a verb, and it applies to 6.

* TEST projection-verb : a projection that gives a verb is a verb
#+begin_src
> f: {x + 1}
{x + 1}
> get[`f], 5
6
> (get[`f]) 5
6
> get[`f] 5
6
> echo[1] 2
1
2
#+end_src

When a term follows a ( ) group or a full projection, the parser runs it first. If its value is a verb, it applies to the term. If not, the two are separate statements.

* TEST early-group-order : a group that a term follows runs when the parser reaches it
#+begin_src
> o: 0
0
> + (o: o + 1) (o: o * 10)
11
> o: 0
0
> (o: o + 1) + (o: o * 10)
1
#+end_src

In the first line, the parse depends on whether =(o: o + 1)= is a verb, so it runs first. In the second line, a dyad follows the group, so the normal right-to-left order holds.
